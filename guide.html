Deadlock Anti-Cheat Guide: How VAC Detection Works

Before you buy or run any Deadlock cheat, it helps to understand how Deadlock anti-cheat and VAC detection actually work. This guide covers the technology behind detection, why deadlock cheaters get caught, the deadlock console commands worth knowing, and why anti-cheat risk is never fully predictable.

What Is Deadlock Anti-Cheat (VAC)?

VAC (Valve Anti-Cheat) is the automated system Valve uses to detect cheat software running alongside Deadlock. It works quietly in the background on official matchmaking servers, scanning for known cheat signatures and suspicious system behavior without interrupting gameplay.

VAC does not ban instantly. Instead, it flags accounts and issues bans in delayed batches — commonly called "VAC waves" — which is why a cheat can appear to "work" for a period before detection catches up. Deadlock's anti-cheat can even flag deadlock cheaters mid-match: during early playtesting Valve added a system that lets the lobby choose between banning a detected cheater immediately or letting them finish the round before the ban lands, so detection is very much a live, active process rather than a silent overnight sweep.

How Cheat Detection Works in Deadlock

Understanding how deadlock cheats enabled in a session actually get flagged also means understanding how detection works, since the two are directly connected. Deadlock cheat detection generally relies on three methods:

1. Signature-Based Detection

VAC scans running processes and memory for known code patterns belonging to public or leaked cheats. This is why free, widely distributed cheats get detected quickly — their signatures are already in VAC's database.

2. Behavioral Detection

Beyond signatures, Deadlock's anti-cheat also looks at behavior: impossible flick speeds, inhuman reaction times, ability usage patterns, or movement across zip-lines and rooftops that doesn't match normal human input can trigger review, even without a known signature match.

3. In-Game Reports & Manual Review

Deadlock also ships a built-in cheat report option, available both in-game and after the match ends, so suspicious deadlock cheaters can be flagged by teammates and opponents for manual review — adding a human layer on top of automated VAC scanning.

Why Players Get Banned in Deadlock

Understanding the common causes can help explain how players get banned in Deadlock:

Using Outdated Or Public Cheats

The single biggest cause of bans is running a cheat that hasn't been updated since the last Deadlock patch. Public, free cheats are reverse-engineered and signature-flagged the fastest.

Using A Non-Stream-Proof Cheat

Streaming or recording with a cheat overlay visible on screen is one of the most common ways players get manually reported and reviewed. Stream-proof mode is designed to limit overlay visibility in OBS and Streamlabs, but you should always test your own setup before streaming.

Account & License Sharing

Sharing a cheat license or Steam account across multiple people increases exposure and the chance of inconsistent, flaggable behavior patterns on one account.
